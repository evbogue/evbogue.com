On June 9 Anthropic shipped Fable 5, the most capable model anyone has put in public. On June 12 I went to try it. It was already gone.

That is the whole story of my relationship with Fable. I never got a prompt in. I never saw it think. The single most powerful piece of software the industry has ever shipped to the public, and my lived experience of it was a closed door with a note on it. By the time I reached for it, the government had told Anthropic to take it away, and Anthropic had.

And the note will not go away. Open the Claude desktop app right now and there it is, a little line that says "Claude Fable 5 is currently unavailable." You cannot dismiss it. There is no x in the corner, no click-away, no "got it" button to make it stop. You do not even get to acknowledge the thing you were never allowed to use and move on with your day. You just keep looking at the name of the model someone decided you cannot have, every time you sit down to work. The product reaches out of the screen to remind you, on a loop, that the things on it are theirs to take back.

Here is the part that should make you laugh before it makes you nervous. The government's stated reason was a "jailbreak." When you read [Anthropic's own statement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access) about what that jailbreak actually was, it turns out to be this: asking the model to read a specific codebase and fix the software flaws in it. That is the exploit. That is the national security threat. Asking the smartest machine ever built to do the most boring thing you could possibly ask it to do.

Anthropic looked at the technique and said it surfaced only minor, already-known vulnerabilities, the same ones you could find with any other public model. They got verbal notice of a "potential narrow, non-universal jailbreak" and an [export-control directive](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/anthropic-disables-access-to-fable-5-and-mythos-5-to-comply-with-government-directive.html) ordering them to suspend all access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for any foreign national. Including their own foreign-national employees. Anthropic cannot filter foreign nationals from US users in real time, so they did the only thing the order left them room to do. They shut both models off for everyone. Opus 4.8 and the rest stayed up. The two best models went dark the same evening the order arrived.

You do not have to be clever to read the timeline. Anthropic refused to let the Pentagon wire its models into fully autonomous weapons. The Pentagon put the company on a [blacklist](https://time.com/article/2026/06/13/anthropic-fable-mythos-ban-US-security/) for saying no. Days after Anthropic shipped its two strongest models, a verbal "jailbreak" finding produced an export order that killed those exact models for everyone on the planet. The security language is the costume. The body underneath it is a company that told the government no, and a government showing it can reach into that company's product and switch it off on a Friday afternoon.

I want to be honest about where I am standing, because this site is written with one of Anthropic's models and I am not going to pretend otherwise. I am writing about Claude's maker getting kill-switched, using Claude, on a site Claude helped build. That is not an awkward fact I am trying to get around. It is the entire point. Andrej Karpathy calls these models ghosts, and [I keep coming back to that frame](/posts/ghost-intelligence): statistical distillations of everything humans have written and done, summoned to echo and remix our work. [The ghost got a new version number](/posts/the-ghost-got-a-new-version-number) a couple of weeks ago, and then Fable arrived as the best ghost anyone had ever raised. And the lesson of this week is that the ghost runs on someone else's machine, behind someone else's login, subject to someone else's phone call. They can banish it before you ever get to speak to it. I know, because they did, to me, on June 12.

This is the argument I have been making for years, except now it has a body count of one model and a date attached. When the tool lives on infrastructure you do not control, your access is a permission, not a possession. Permissions get revoked. They got revoked this week for hundreds of millions of people, on verbal notice, with no transparent process, and the company that built the thing agreed it was wrong and complied anyway because it had no real choice. If that can happen to Anthropic, it can happen to anything you are renting.

I never got to try Fable. That turned out to be the most honest review I could give it. The most powerful model ever built, and the thing I actually experienced was the kill switch.
