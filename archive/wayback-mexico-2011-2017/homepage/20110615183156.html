Experiments

In my experience, I can only know a thing once I’ve done it. This is why I conduct experiments in order to see if a thing will actually work — or if I’ll even enjoy the experience.

One of the most important elements of experiments is being willing to fail, as well as being willing to give them up once I’ve achieved them.

In a way, I’m my own guinea pig as I explore the world.

On this living data page, I’m recording some of the more notable lifestyle experiments that I’ve conducted. I’m including a brief overview of the experience, how long it lasted, and whether or not the experiment is currently in effect.

The experiments are in three categories:

1. Intentions. These are experiments that I’m attempting to implement.
2. Implemented. These are experiments that are in progress until further notice.
3. Untethers. These are experiments in the art of subtraction.

Start your own. Write about your own experiments, overview, intention, and results. Link back here. I’d love to hear about your projects.

Intentions

Digital Sabbatical

Overview: In our always-on culture, it’s important to step away. That’s why I’m taking the entire month of July off of the Internet. I won’t be answering emails, reading online, or anything else that involves plugging in.

I will only be publishing Letters. If you want to read what I discover in a disconnected world, you’ll need to subscribe.

Intention: To check in with myself, and rediscover the world, without the Internet as an endless distraction.

Length: Month of July 2011

More: Insights from last year’s digital sabbatical.

Eliminate Email from Life

Overview: Email is an antiquated technology, invented over 40 years ago when the only thing we could compare it to was the mailman. Now, email has become a world-wide obligation. Many people sit in their inboxes all day, clicking refresh, waiting to hit the next reply. I want to opt-out.

Questions: Why do many people feel like they can email anyone in the world asking for help? Why do so many people feel obligated to reply to everyone? Is ‘staying in touch’ useful anyway? What is the future of communication?

Intention: To never email again.

Length of time: I would like to implement by January 2012.

Results: TBA

More: Email is Your Sanctuary, Email Charter by TED Chris.

Secret Yoga Teacher Training (SYTT ’11)

Overview: After completing my 200 hour Yoga Alliance certification at the end of 2010, I decided it was time to take the next leg of the journey “off the record”. So, I enrolled in SYTT ’11. No one knows who else is enrolled, where the classes are held or exactly what we’ll be learning every day except for participant(s).

Intentions: To explore my practice in a self-directed style.

Length: June 2011

Results (so far): Leveled up my Yin Yoga practice. Intensive “Yoga on rocks” master classes. Infiltrated hot yoga classes. Does EFT actually work? …more to come.

 

Implemented

Live with less than 100 things

Overview: In July of 2009, I decided to live with less than 100 things. I threw out all of my stuff and booked a flight across the country with everything in a bag. Currently, my Things are listed here. I don’t count them anymore, but you’re welcome to. My old blog Far Beyond The Stars was partially about my experience living with less.

Intention: to see what I actually needed to live my life.

Length: July 2009 – Present

Results: Location independence, intense feelings of freedom, clearer priorities, lifestyle akin to early retirement achieved at the age of 25.

Freedom from owning a car


Overview: Owning a car is a tether. I love driving, but I didn’t want to own a car. If I wanted to move across the country, I’d need to either sell my car, park it somewhere safe, or who knows what. It stresses me out just thinking about it. So, I’ve never owned a car. Instead, I use Zipcar for day trips and vacations as necessary. These days, I’d never dream of owning a car for any reason.

Intention: Mobility outside cities occasionally, without obligation of owning a car or paying insurance and gas.

Length: 2003 – Present

Results: For me, owning a car is unnecessary when living in major urban centers.

Location Independence

Overview: In 2009, I decided that I wanted to live be able to live and work from anywhere. I’d read a book called The 4 Hour Work Week, which gave me a lot of ideas about the possibility of living from anywhere. However, as with any idea, they tend to stay that way until I do them. So, I decided to give it a try. I quit my job, put all of my Things into a bag, and hopped onto a plane to Portland Oregon with everything in a bag.

Intention: To live and work from anywhere.

Length: August 2009 – Present

Results: Ongoing success. I craft for the web from my Macbook Air. I haven’t set foot in an office in nearly two years.

More: How I Live and Work from Anywhere, Minimalist Business.

Check Email Once Per Day

Overview: Having a public email address led to my receiving far too many emails per day. I pretended like I was able to deal with the onslaught, but in actuality I wasn’t able to reply intelligently to any of the emails I was receiving. Instead of rolling over and dying, I decided to fight back. First: I only checked my email once per day. Eventually I found that I couldn’t give my email address away to the public anymore, so I took my email address off my website. I only give my email to close friends, peers, and subscribers to The Letter — because I know they’ll only contact me with intention.

Intention: to survive onslaught of email.

Length: March 2010 – Present

Results: Feel no obligation to return emails from most people anymore.

More: Email is My Sanctuary, Minimalist Business.

Untethers

Quit Facebook

Overview: I began to notice in late 2010 that my interactions on Facebook were keeping me from being present in my own life. I knew what everyone else was doing, except the person across the table from me. I had 1,000+ friends, and couldn’t figure out where they’d all come from. Dunbar’s law states that a human mind can only have 150 connections, so I knew something was wrong. Facebook’s “Like” function kept drawing me back into the application, distracting me from my own life. So, I decided to quit.

Intention: Quit Facebook in order to be more present in my own life.

Length: Initiated in Dec 2010 –> Indefinitely.

Results: Many extra hours in my life to be present with the people who are actually in my life.
