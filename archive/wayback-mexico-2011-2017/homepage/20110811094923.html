How I Write Books and Blog Posts

Written by Ev Bogue | Follow me on Google+

This is the first in a multi-part series on how I write books and blog posts. If you’re interested, sign up to receive free updates from this blog in your inbox or via RSS.

//

This is how I write on my computer:

I use Scrivener to write everything now. ($45, with a free 30-day trial)

The team at Literature and Latte have been incredibly on the ball with updates to this App.

Using Mac OS Lion, I put Scrivener in full screen mode. In this way, I don’t get distracted by the rest of the things going on in my computer.

My computer is an entrance to the dream world of the Internet. Scrivener is my own private world which isn’t quite in the dream. It’s going deep, but just with my internal self and the canvas of the typed page.

I organize all of my writing into folders in a Scrivener Document called “Notebook”. I have an icon on my desktop (it’s one of only a few icons on my desktop) which leads directly into Notebook. These folders change.

When I want to write something for the blog, I create a blank document in my ‘Blog Drafts’ folder.

When I want to write something for Evolving Your Digital Work, I create a blank document in my ‘Letter’ folder.

Then I start writing.

Sometimes I don’t have time to write, so I’ll just create a document with a headline. Then, if I still care about the idea later, I can write it then.

When something is published. I right click on the text document in the column on the left, and mark as ‘published’.

I have another scrivener document on my desktop called ‘Untether’. This is where I’m booking my next book. It is dedicated exclusively to Untether (more about the the Untether project). If I write something for Untether by accident in my Notebook, I only need to copy and paste it over. I keep these separate, because Untether is a separate project from my notebook.

//

What is your workflow like for writing? I’d love to hear in the comments.

Experiments in Google+ (and 150 Free Invites)

Written by Ev Bogue | Follow me on Google+

(Invite link to Google+ is here)

I’ve been on Google+ for a few weeks now. I’ve been off Twitter for two months, and off Facebook for nine months.

I’m beginning to believe that Google+ is the most useful social network that’s evolved so far. I’m seeing high levels of engagement on the service. Google has done an amazing job in how the service is structured. The service has been created in a way that makes sense to a wide variety of people using in many different ways.

In a way, it’s a social layer that sits on top of the Internet.

//

Why Google+ is different.

In my experience, Google+ works differently than Twitter and Facebook.

In the words of techno philosopher Kevin Kelly “Often learning a new tool requires unlearning the old one. The habits of using a land line phone don’t work in email or cell phone. The habits of email don’t work in twitter. The habits of twitter won’t work in what is next.”

I believe Twitter and Google+ are fundamentally different. I see a lot of people using Google+ like it’s Twitter, which doesn’t work.

What does work on Google+? How is it different? We’re going to find that out. Unlike Facebook, it’s public. Unlike Twitter, content is longer-form and conversation based.

I like how there’s no need to unfollow people for the service to work properly for users who have large followings like myself (I have well over 1,000 followers at this point, and rising.) I can reciprocate by following back, but it’s easy to filter my stream at the same time in order to focus.

//

I asked on Google+ how many social networks people were on, and why they were on them.

Many people said they were on the three big ones. Facebook, Twitter, and Google+. Not many are on LinkedIn, because I don’t really write or hang for the professional crowd — (or even understand them much really.)

One of the number one reasons for not leaving Facebook was “I’m waiting for my friends to move over.”

I don’t believe many of my friends are going to move over from Facebook. Just like when we graduated high school and 95% of everyone stayed in high school, so will 95% of everyone stay on Facebook. They’ve bought into a service that owns their content, and won’t let it be exported.

In my experience, most people on Facebook don’t have a foothold on the open web (they’re not publishers.) So, they’ll probably stay there. This is okay. There’s a huge new and growing community of amazing people on Google+. This is a time to connect with new people.

I worry too many people are trying to divide their attention between three social Apps, and ending up with very low results. If your intention is to find new customers, then in my experience a more focused approach is probably best.

I’m only maintaining a presence on Google+ now.

The purpose of this is ultimately to bring people back to my site, it’s the home base. I’m a professional writer, so I have to constantly think about where the support from the work comes from. Am I just seeing traffic, or am I seeing cash flow? These are all open questions in my mind.

//

Anyway, these are just a few of my thoughts. There are no solid answers here, I’m simply exploring what this next evolution in social means.

How are you experimenting with using Google+? Let us know in the comments.

Why I Untethered from Minimalist Labels

Written by Ev Bogue | Follow me on Google+

Stefan Reist asked this question on Google+.

“…how do you communicate and explain your minimalistic lifestyle to your immediate loved ones (family, partner, kids) when they somewhat (or fully?) disagree with your lifestyle?”

This is an excellent question. Here’s the response that I answered on this thread in Google+.

“I don’t really use the term minimalist anymore, because it’s a label and people get attached to that. Instead, I try to use an action term. What I’ve come up with is “untether”.

Instead of labeling myself a minimalist, as people get attached to that and say “He’s a minimalist!” It’s all around the action. I’ve found that it’s easier to explain to others when the focus is a specific action, and not a philosophy and label. I didn’t always think this way, and it got me into a lot of trouble when I labeled myself and preached philosophy. Close people thought I might be judging them for having more things than me. That’s why I don’t label or philosophize anymore.

For example, when I left San Francisco in February, I untethered from anything that would have held me in that place. I had purchased a bed, a lot of kitchen stuff like a blender, knives cutting board. I just let it all go, because I realized that holding onto that stuff would cause me much more trouble in the long term. My roommates at the time were grateful for me leaving the stuff to them, because it was an upgrade. Otherwise, I would have given them to Salvo or some other charitable organization.

It helps to tell from experience. +Gwen Bell does her best to always tell from experience on her blog, and I aspire to do the same. When I tell from experience about an action. “I untethered from wearing jeans, because they were uncomfortable and yoga pants are much more flexible. Now I have two pairs of yoga pants. It’s really enjoyable to me.” I show from example that I explored that experience and found it beneficial.

Last year I untethered from almost everything, getting down to 47 things, and I found that most of the things I thought I needed weren’t necessary. So? I live that way still.

It helps to avoid the labels though, that was the key tension with others in my experience.”

How to Untether from “Alright”

Written by Ev Bogue | Follow me on Google+

I get asked this a lot:

“Why would you ever…?”

Leave a city that you love so much.
Delete a best selling e-book that still sells copies every day.
Quit a job with a fancy title.
Stop eating something that tastes good.
Donate a perfectly good jacket because you I have two.

Why? Because it isn’t a challenge anymore. I’m covering the same ground again and again. I’m taking 25% of my energy and investing it in time that has already eclipsed. That leaves me with only 75% for the new stuff. As more things build up in the past, more energy gets dedicated to maintenance. Pretty soon I’m maintaining with 100%, which equals stagnation.

I don’t want to maintain connections, I want to grow the existing, or create new ones. This applies to everything in life.

I untether to evolve.

Growing Mindfully

Written by Ev Bogue | Follow me on Google+

Last year my old blog, Far Beyond The Stars, topped out at 80,000 visitors a month. Now I’m at a slowly rising 18,534 readers. This smaller number is understandable, as I took about 6 months off from blogging regularly as I was (and am) still working with the deeper layer of the premium letter, Evolving Your Digital Work.

Now, I’m growing the blog again. This time, I want to do it slowly.

It’s easy to grow a blog too quickly.

Throw in a cleaver headline, or trigger the hell out of people, and I’m liable to have a viral post on my hands. Two years ago, I got very good at writing just such posts. Last holiday season I wrote a post called “How to Save $1,300 and be Loved by Everyone Forever”, which went viral, was retweeted multi-hundreds of times, and seen by tens of thousands. It was about consuming mindfully during the holidays.

While in practice, more seems better. In actuality, more isn’t necessarily.

The thing about a viral post, especially one with a trick headline is many of the people coming didn’t necessarily want to read my blog. They just came because there was a crowd, so what must have been happening had to have been important.

My plan this time around is to grow the blog, the premium Letter, and my next e-book mindfully.

I’m not going to be writing viral headlines, sucking up to super-popular bloggers, or selling my soul for eyeballs this time around.

This means I need your help.

If you read a post, and like it. I’d appreciate it if…

…at the very least, tap the +1 button. It helps with Google search rankings, social proof, and other magic. It’s very easy to do, at no cost to you.

…share it! On the primary network you maintain your connections on (in this day in age, that could be many places or one.)

…link to it from your blog. This is the best, but understandably, also the most difficult. I tend to check my trackbacks, and enjoy reading your blog posts.

Thanks for your help.

Best,
Ev
