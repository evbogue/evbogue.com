Taskmaster

September 03, 2014

When I’m working on a new product, I’m ruthless with my time.

There are three stages to a launch.

  1. Generate work
  2. Beta test
  3. Public launch
  4. Reach out

In the first phase, I’m an introvert writing maniac. I need to generate as much as work as possible on the subject. I begin by writing down chapter titles into my Moleskine notebook, with the Muji pen I bought in Japan two years ago. Now it’s leaking ink into the cap. I write as many chapter titles as possible on two pages. These pages face each other. If I can fill two pages worth of chapter titles that I feel confident about, I know I have an entire product.

Next I self-isolate. I plug in the headphones. I go to a Starbucks where I’m surrounded by people, but I talk to no one. I give myself one hour. I pick a chapter. I write it. I pick the next chapter. I write it. I take a break to smoke a cigarillo. Delicados are only $24 pesos in la Ciudad. In Australia Cigarettes are $20 AU a pack now! Then I write the third chapter. Sometimes I’ll test some of these chapters in public to see if I get any response to them – eith enthusiastic or condomnation. Either is good. If I get no response, I know I’m doing something wrong.

The next phase is the beta test phase. I reach out to a handful of people who’ve communicated with me. I record email addresses and Bitmessage address into my Gitodex (the modern equivilent of a Rolodex). Once I’ve opened a line of communication with some recent people who’ve been in touch, I ask them what they think of the new product I’m working on. Sometimes this leads to sales, other times it doesn’t. If I can’t sell a product on the backchannel, then most of the time I’m smart enough to scrap the product and try a different topic. Sometimes (especially these days) products sell better on the backchannel than they do on the front channel.

The next phase is my public launch. In some cases this can come very fast, or I can keep a product on the back channel until I feel that it’s good enough, or people are enthusiastic enough about it, for it to go public. I might combine this with an initial discount on the price to get momentum going on sales.

The final phase is the worst. This is the reaching out phase. This means cold calling people. It means having in-depth conversations about why the product might benefit the person on the other end of the line. This is the Carcetti (a politician from The Wire) phase, where I lock myself into a room and have conversations with as many people as possible to try and make the case for how the product will benefit the person on the other end of the line.

With both of the lock-in phases, I try to limit the amount of time I spend doing the hardest work. If I work too hard, or work all day, it can lead to burnout – which can make it impossible to repeat the process again in the near future – if that’s what I need to do to get the perfect product in front of my readers.

If I’m lucky, the product just sells itself.

But most of the time it’s fucking hard work. But what work isn’t hard work?
