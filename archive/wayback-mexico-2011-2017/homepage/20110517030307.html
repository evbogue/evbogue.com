Why did I write Minimalist Business?

To answer a specific question question that my audience kept returning to: how was I able to start a zero overhead online business that runs itself?

A bit of history: in July of 2009, I quit my day job as a photo editor at New York Magazine and jumped on a plane to Portland Oregon with all of my stuff in a bag. By February, I’d created a Minimalist Business that supported my lifestyle anywhere in the world.

Fast-forward to today, I’m still grateful for the work that I put in last year, building the foundation that allows me to travel or relocate whenever I want.

I recently finished a three-city tour of the US, uprooting from a retreat in Boulder, CO. I visited New York for a little over two weeks, gathering valuable information for my next project, dropped by Chicago, before heading west. I’m currently a few days into setting up shop in a city that’s almost completely new to me: Seattle, WA.

How am I able to live and work from anywhere?

1. I reduced padding in my life for complete mobility.

2. I reduced business overhead to near zero.

3. I used existing infrastructure to automate distribution.

Obviously, there’s a lot more to it. That’s why the book is 125 pages + a 65 page additional PDF added at the end of 2010. The upgraded edition has a 30 page quick start guide — with step-by-step instructions for those just getting started online.

Preview the first 37 pages here.

Creating a minimalist business has allowed me to:

1. Reduce meaningless work.

I currently work less than 2 hours a week on admin and communications. This allows me to focus on the work that challenges me: experiencing the world and telling my experiences on The Letter.

2. Focus on improving myself.

I studied for my 200 hour yoga certification at the end of last year, and I’ve been able to invest hundreds of hours improving my skills as a writer.

3. Enjoy my life in the world.

For three years I sat in a cubicle, waiting for my life to start. It didn’t, until I left. Since, I’ve lived in 5 different American cities, and learned more about myself and my world than I ever had previously. I couldn’t have done this without the skills I learned during the first year, and wrote down with a raw fury into Minimalist Business early last year.

To see how I’m exploring my world, follow me on Instagr.am (@evbogue).

I hope the work can help you do the same.
